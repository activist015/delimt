# The Psychology of Bad Habits (And Why Willpower Is a Lie)

Everyone talks about willpower like it's a muscle. Train it. Build it. Use it.

The science says something very different.

---

## The Depletion Problem

In 1998, Roy Baumeister at Case Western ran a now-famous experiment. Two groups. One resisted eating cookies. The other didn't. Then both were given unsolvable puzzles.

The cookie-resisters gave up significantly faster.

**Willpower is finite.** Every act of self-control draws from the same limited pool. By evening — after a full day of decisions, distractions, and discipline — that pool is nearly empty. Which is exactly when doom-scrolling, impulse buying, and texting your ex feel completely irresistible.

This is called **ego depletion**. And it explains why your worst habits always hit at the same time of day.

---

## The Habit Loop

Charles Duhigg's research on habit formation identifies three components:

- **Cue** — the trigger that starts the behaviour
- **Routine** — the behaviour itself
- **Reward** — the feeling that reinforces it

Most apps try to interrupt the routine. They block the app. They hide the website.

But the cue — boredom, anxiety, loneliness, procrastination — is still there. And your brain will find another routine to deliver the same reward. Usually a worse one.

**To break a habit, you have to interrupt at the cue. Not the routine.**
